## What is a premise to Gervais? Subject vs. target ("the joke is about X, the target is Y")

### Takeaway
Gervais's own stated theory, repeated almost word-for-word from about 2016 to 2025, is that every joke has a **subject**, the taboo thing named on the surface, and a separate **target**, the thing actually ridiculed. The target is often a prejudice, a hypocrisy, a stupid assumption, or his own persona. Offence, he says, comes from people who "mistake the subject of the joke with the actual target." For a writer, his premise is the ironic distance between the two.

### Inferences
- **Working premise formula:** *"[Taboo subject] is the bait. [Someone's stupidity, hypocrisy or prejudice, often my own on-stage character's] is the meat."* A Gervais premise is rarely an observation like "airports are weird." It is an attitude towards a moral posture, for example "people who get offended on behalf of others are self-regarding," "religious literalism is absurd when read aloud literally," or "celebrities preaching virtue are hypocrites."
- The subject is chosen for **voltage** (AIDS, the Holocaust, cancer, dying children, trans issues, the Bible). The target is chosen for **defensibility**: an idiot, a hypocrite, a bigot, or the audience's own assumption. That pairing lets him go dark while claiming moral cover.
- To write in his mode, state both explicitly before drafting: "Subject: ___. Target: ___." If the target cannot be named, the joke is just shock and falls outside his mode, whatever critics say about his own misses.

## The persona: the arrogant/ignorant "idiot," mock-pomposity and faux-logical argument

### Takeaway
The "Ricky Gervais" on stage is a character: a smug, faux-humble, self-satisfied celebrity who is confidently wrong, petty and vain. Many laughs land on *him*. The model comes straight from David Brent, a comedy of the gap between how a man sees himself and how we see him. His faux-logic (rational argument used to reach a monstrous or petty conclusion) is the main engine.

### Cited Findings
- ABC News described his stage act as a performance, not conventional stand-up: a "self-satisfied, faux-humble celebrity" character — [ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/Television/story?id=6257885)
- Gervais insists the stage figure is a persona even though it uses his name, clothes and voice. A profile notes that, unlike Al Murray's Pub Landlord, "it isn't immediately obvious that Gervais is playing a character," and calls the act closer to comic acting than stand-up. He has voiced concern that the satire of prejudice can be misread — [rickygervais.com interview, Mar 2008](https://www.rickygervais.com/interview_mar08.php)
- The Scotsman groups his stand-up persona with Brent and Andy Millman (*Extras*) as unintentionally arrogant characters — [Scotsman](https://www.scotsman.com/news/ricky-gervais-interview-i-get-no-joy-from-seeing-my-fat-face-on-the-telly-although-i-dont-think-anyone-believes-me-on-that-2472167)
- **[V]** On Brent: "we're still laughing about the difference between how Brent sees himself and how we see him." — [Rotten Tomatoes](https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/ricky-gervais-on-the-office-movie-and-the-new-breed-of-narcissism)
- **[V]** "I love the comedy of embarrassment; I absolutely love it." His writing question for a character: "what's the worst thing for this chap to say?" — [World Screen, 2006](https://worldscreen.com/?p=86465)
- **[V]** "I used David Brent to show what bad comedy is: catchphrases, begging for laughs, being too desperate." — [Rolling Stone Q&A](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/qa-ricky-gervais-230346)
- In Deadline he explains the Brent audience cycle. They laugh at his pomposity, then sympathise when he is humiliated — [Deadline, 2016](https://deadline.com/2016/06/ricky-gervais-celebrity-religion-special-correspondents-office-extras-1201777363/)
- A critical Hollywood Reporter piece contrasts Derek, who has no gap between self-image and how we see him, with Brent, whose blind spots "fueled the comedy" — [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com//news/ricky-gervais-at-home-god-609708)
- A reviewer describes his method as a **"pull-back and reveal"**: he leads the audience down a dark path, then reveals "a surprisingly logical, if twisted, conclusion" — [Comedy In Your Eye](https://www.comedyinyoureye.com/post/ricky-gervais-stand-up-comedy-gigs-shows-and-tours-in-london-uk-and-europe)
- **Faux-logic on obesity (Science, 2009/10).** The transcript has him saying he has never attacked overweight people, only pointed out that weight gain comes from eating more than you burn. Then: **[V]** "even though it is their own fault – and it is their own fault – I feel sorry for them." — snippet of [Scraps from the Loft: Science transcript](https://scrapsfromtheloft.com/comedy/ricky-gervais-live-iv-science-transcript/); show described as "rants and tongue-in-cheek political incorrectness about... religion, racism and obesity" — [York Vision review](https://www.yorkvision.co.uk/archived/ricky-gervais-science/15/12/2009)
- He also turns the obesity logic on himself in his own voice. **[V]** Tweet: "Stop fat shaming me! It's not my fault that eating and drinking too much makes me put on weight. It's science." — [X/Twitter, 2020](https://x.com/rickygervais/status/1300768698895040512)

### Inferences
- **Persona rules for a writer:**
  1. **Status inflation.** He speaks as a rich, famous, clever man who is above you, then gets caught being petty (blocking a nut-allergy mum, bragging about charity).
  2. **Confident wrongness.** The character delivers the "rational" position with total certainty, and the extremity or pettiness of the conclusion is the laugh.
  3. **Fake humility.** He offers false modesty and visible self-congratulation ("I do a lot for charity...") and the audience sees through it.
  4. **Self as fall guy.** Fat, old, vain, lazy. The obesity "it's their own fault" logic sits alongside his own belly, and that keeps the cruelty partly self-directed.
- **Faux-logic template:** Begin with a premise everyone agrees with ("weight comes from calories," "a joke has a subject," "the Bible says X literally"). Take each step at reasonable speed. Arrive at a conclusion that is logically consistent but socially monstrous or absurd. Then hold the face, or giggle.
- The Brent DNA counts for a lot. His stand-up character is a Brent who knows he's on stage. Write the line the character thinks is a winning argument, and let the audience laugh at the gap.

### Gaps
- No primary-source quote was found in which Gervais literally says "I'm playing an idiot" about his stand-up persona. The ABC and rickygervais.com sources establish the persona idea, but the exact phrase is unconfirmed.

## Bit breakdowns by special

### Takeaway
Across specials the architecture repeats. A taboo subject is announced, he names the "rule" he is supposedly breaking, an anecdote (often a Twitter complaint) or a text read literally sets up a faux-logic argument, the reveal shows the true target, and then he stacks tags, frequently a meta tag explaining why you laughed.

### Cited Findings and breakdowns

#### A. *Humanity* (Netflix, 2018). Chortle called the touring version his strongest stand-up and said "even the liberal-baiting is more nuanced" ([Wikipedia summary of Guardian/Brian Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Gervais:_Humanity)). Subjects include "rape, paedophiles, sickening serial killers, Aids, fatal allergies, the Holocaust and transgender issues" ([Chortle](https://www.chortle.co.uk/review/2017/06/16/36824/ricky_gervais:_humanity)).

**A1. The nut-allergy Twitter bit**
- *Premise (attitude):* People who take offence online are self-centred and selectively outraged, and I'm a petty enough celebrity to fight them.
- *Setup:* He tells of a joke about nuts (it began on Fallon; see [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/video/2774343/ricky-gervais-receives-social-media-backlash-after-joking-about-killing-someone-with-a-peanut-allergy)), then reads hostile tweets aloud in a whiny voice: **[V]** "My daughter's nut-intolerant. How dare you?" He adds the aside "Who is this woman? She's tweeted 15..." — [clip.cafe](https://clip.cafe/ricky-gervais-humanity-2018/my-daughters-nut-intolerant-nbsp-how-dare-you/)
- *Act-out:* **[V]** "Dear Mr. Gervais, I have a fatal nut allergy." *Delete.* The punch is the one-word action, said deadpan — [clip.cafe](https://clip.cafe/ricky-gervais-humanity-2018/and-went-dear-mr-gervais-i-have-a-fatal-nut-allergy/)
- *Turn / escalation:* A hypothetical plane scenario: **[V]** "A lady's getting on who's so allergic, even someone eating nuts nearby would cause her to..." — [clip.cafe](https://clip.cafe/ricky-gervais-humanity-2018/a-ladys-getting-on-whos-allergic/). Then the persona's monstrous solution and blame-shift: **[V]** "And I go, 'Who's been eating nuts?' I get off scot-free." — [clip.cafe](https://clip.cafe/ricky-gervais-humanity-2018/and-i-go-whos-been-eating-nuts/)
- *Meta tag / reveal of target:* He rebuts complainers by pointing to what they *did* laugh at. In his own retelling: **[V]** "Yeah, but you laughed about AIDS, the Holocaust…" — [Men's Journal](https://www.mensjournal.com/entertainment/ricky-gervais-opens-up-about-netflix-show-humanity-religion-and-more); [RTÉ Guide via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/ireland/rte-guide/20190311/283893049351785). The target becomes the complainer's selective morality: you're only offended when it's *your* topic.
- *Why it works:* Three stacked laughs come from three mechanisms. The read-aloud voice mocks the complainer. "Delete" is a pure status move with minimal words. Getting away with murder is persona villainy taken to an extreme. The meta turn ("you laughed at AIDS") implicates the audience, so the joke's real butt is selective offence, including the audience's own.

**A2. Caitlyn Jenner / "the subject is stereotypes"**
- *Premise:* My Globes joke wasn't transphobic, and I'll prove it by dissecting it in front of you.
- *Structure:* Over 15 minutes the bit re-explains the 2016 Globes joke ([PinkNews](https://www.thepinknews.com/2026/01/12/ricky-gervais-golden-globes-trans-community-comments-lgbtq-controversy/)). The original Globes joke **[P-mem]**: Jenner "has done a lot for transgender people... a role model... broken down barriers, destroyed stereotypes... She didn't do a lot for women drivers." The setup is three sincere-sounding compliments, then a flip to a pivot (the car crash / women drivers) that changes the target.
- *Meta dissection:* **[V]** "The subject of that joke is stereotypes. That's… the target of the joke, just so we're clear." It closes with **[V]** "And that is why this joke is not transphobic" — [Entertainment Voice](https://entertainmentvoice.com/2018/03/13/nothing-is-sacred-for-ricky-gervais-in-netflix-special-humanity/)
- *Escalation:* A faux-logical reductio about self-identification, comparing it to identifying as a chimp — [PinkNews](https://www.thepinknews.com/2026/01/12/ricky-gervais-golden-globes-trans-community-comments-lgbtq-controversy/); criticised by [Indiewire](https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/ricky-gervais-netflix-transphobic-jenner-1201938675/)
- *Why it works (and where it fails):* Explaining the joke becomes the act. The lecture-professor voice is mock-pompous, and the audience laughs at the pedantry and at being "taught." Critics argue the reductio's real target slides onto trans people, which shows how fragile the subject/target defence is. A writer should treat this as a cautionary example: the target has to survive a hostile paraphrase.

**A3. "I make jokes about AIDS, cancer, famine, the Holocaust"** (list-as-credential)
- The transcript includes a medical-detection-dog bit (**[V]** "There's medical detection dogs that can smell if you've got… AIDS.") and a later passage listing taboo subjects he jokes about — [Chortle snippet / transcript](https://scrapsfromtheloft.com/comedy/ricky-gervais-humanity-transcript/)
- *Mechanism:* The **dog-sniffing joke** is a literalism engine. Take a sincere science fact (dogs detect disease) and push it into a social scenario where the dog's behaviour (sniffing, reacting) becomes a public accusation. Laughter comes from the image, while the stated subject stays "science."

#### B. *Science* (2009/2010) and *Out of England 2* (2010): the Bible-reading / literalism bits
- Reviewers single out Out of England 2's "Book of Noah" reading, the "fat segment," and "Flying after 9/11" as highlights. Science shares the Noah's Ark material, including the "two of every kind" routine. Animals (2003) already had a "bible segment," a reading of a children's Noah book — [IMDb: Animals](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0397612/); [MetaFilter](https://www.metafilter.com/55612/Ricky-Gervais-reads-the-Bible); [tomorrowstarted.com](https://www.tomorrowstarted.com/2024/09/ricky-gervais-book-of-noah/.html)

**B1. Noah's Ark read aloud** (structure from sources, specific lines **[P-mem]**)
- *Premise:* If you read a sacred text the way you'd read any other story, it collapses into nonsense.
- *Setup:* He reads a children's Bible (sometimes the actual book) in a solemn storyteller voice, the sincere register.
- *Turn:* He stops after each sentence and interrogates it literally, like a pedantic schoolboy or science teacher. How did two of *every* animal fit? How did the kangaroos get there from Australia, and the penguins? What did the lions eat? [P-mem]
- *Tags:* Each interruption is a new laugh. The sentence-plus-literal-question unit repeats and escalates in absurdity (logistics, then biology, then morality: God drowns everyone, including children and animals).
- *Why it works:* It's a reliable engine (text, then literal question) that produces laughs at an even pace, and the target (literalist faith) is defensible. The persona plays teacher-with-a-child, a mock-pompous status. The religious subject is the voltage, and the logic is the weapon.

**B2. Obesity faux-logic** (Science)
- *Premise:* Fatness is a matter of thermodynamics, and excuses are lies. My compassion is real and also condescending.
- *Lines:* He claims he never attacked overweight people, only pointed out the energy-balance fact; then **[V]** "even though it is their own fault – and it is their own fault – I feel sorry for them." — [Science transcript snippet](https://scrapsfromtheloft.com/comedy/ricky-gervais-live-iv-science-transcript/)
- *Mechanics:* The **repetition-as-reveal** ("and it is their own fault") is the laugh. The disclaimer clause is supposed to soften, and instead the repetition shows the persona can't stop himself. Sincerity, flip, then the character exposing his own smugness. He is visibly overweight himself, and that undercuts the cruelty (compare the 2020 tweet above).

#### C. *SuperNature* (Netflix, 2022). Netflix billed it as his take on "the rules of comedy, spoiling his cats and how super actual nature is" — [Wikipedia-style summary via search](https://www.chortle.co.uk/review/2022/05/24/50845/ricky_gervais%3A_supernature)

**C1. Opening "irony" lesson / rules of comedy**
- *Premise:* Audiences don't understand irony, so I'll explain it first, and the explanation is the license for everything that follows.
- *Structure:* He opens by explaining irony to the audience, telling them that he will sometimes say things he doesn't mean. A Substack writer claims the opening is "almost word-for-word plagiarism" of a Stewart Lee bit; this is a single unverified opinion — [Substack: "Ricky Gervais broke my heart"](https://shootingmovies.substack.com/p/ricky-gervais-broke-my-heart)
- *Why it works:* It's pre-emptive meta-framing. The disclaimer is itself a joke (smug lecturing) and also a contract with the audience. Every later dark line can now get a laugh twice, once on content and once on "remember, irony."

**C2. "Old-fashioned women"**
- *Premise (as he frames it):* Absolutist ideological language produces absurd verbal contortions.
- *Setup:* A "not all women" qualifier, then a split into "old-fashioned" and "new" women. Punch: **[V]** "The old-fashioned women, the ones with wombs." Then it escalates to bathroom-access debates — [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2022/05/24/entertainment/ricky-gervais-supernature-trans-jokes-netflix-cec/index.html); [PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/ricky-gervais-supernature-transphobia-2657387703.html); [Variety](https://variety.com/2022/tv/news/ricky-gervais-trans-jokes-netflix-special-supernature-1235275966/)
- *Mechanics:* A **mock-progressive euphemism**. The persona adopts the cautious, inclusive register ("not all women") and then picks the most loaded possible clarifier, delivered as though helpful. The laugh comes from the incongruity of register (polite, retro "old-fashioned," as if discussing furniture) and content (biology).
- *Contested target:* He claims the target is "trans activist ideology" ([Telegraph](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-daily-telegraph/20220526/281633898858264)). GLAAD called the material "anti-trans rants masquerading as jokes" ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2022/05/24/netflix-ricky-gervais-transphobic-jokes)). CNN reports he says he supports trans rights in real life but "makes fun of everything" ([CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2022/05/24/entertainment/ricky-gervais-supernature-trans-jokes-netflix-cec/index.html)).

**C3. The taboo-list and AIDS/Africa joke**
- He lists the subjects he jokes about ("AIDS, famine, cancer, the Holocaust, rape, pedophilia"), then argues that trans people are the group he's told he can't joke about. One critic called the AIDS/Africa joke the special's "most potentially offensive" bit — [Awards Daily](https://www.awardsdaily.com/2022/06/02/why-critics-should-not-review-ricky-gervais-supernature/); [PinkNews](https://www.thepinknews.com/2022/05/25/ricky-gervais-supernature-trans-aids/)
- *Mechanism:* The **list** works as both setup and argument. The rhythm of escalating horrors gets laughs on its own (each item worse), and the final item is framed as the absurd exception. Cumulative listing builds momentum for a contrast punch.

#### D. *Armageddon* (Netflix, Dec 25, 2023). Road-tested in work-in-progress dates ([Beyond The Joke](https://beyondthejoke.co.uk/content/12531/ricky-gervais-tickets); [Substack warm-up review](https://sknight.substack.com/p/comedy-review-ricky-gervaiss-armageddon))

**D1. Make-A-Wish video messages**
- *Premise:* My charity work is real, but I'm such a vain, tactless celebrity that I'd do it terribly.
- *Setup (sincere, with a hedge):* **[V]** "I've been doing video messages recently for terminally ill children – and only if they request it, obviously." The "obviously" already gets a laugh, because it imagines him forcing himself on dying kids — [Yahoo UK](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/ricky-gervais-responds-backlash-joke-151353300.html)
- *Denial-tag (paralipsis):* He says he didn't burst into hospitals going **[V]** "Wake up, baldy!" Denying the horrible thing lets him say the horrible thing — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/entertainment/ricky-gervais-slams-critics-netflix-special-petition-demands-removal-joke.amp)
- *Punch:* He always opens the video the same way: **[V]** "Why didn't you wish to get better?" — [Today](https://www.today.com/popculture/tv/ricky-gervais-responds-controversial-joke-netflix-special-rcna130753); [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2023/12/ricky-gervais-responds-petition-asking-joke-removal-netflix-armageddon-special-1235680591/)
- *Meta tag:* **[V]** "These are all jokes, alright?" Then a riff on a harsher word, a mock-reassurance that becomes another laugh — [Today](https://www.today.com/popculture/tv/ricky-gervais-responds-controversial-joke-netflix-special-rcna130753)
- *Why it works:* "Why didn't you wish to get better?" is **faux-logic in one sentence**. It treats a wish literally, as an optimisation problem, and the persona's tone-deaf rationality is the butt. Gervais dismissed "99 per cent" of the backlash as "faux offence" ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/ricky-gervais-criticises-calls-joke-103514015.html)). Response was mixed. Variety called the special "Not Controversial, Just Boring" ([Variety](https://variety.com/2023/tv/news/ricky-gervais-armageddon-review-netflix-1235851313/)).

#### E. Golden Globes monologues (2010, 2011, 2012, 2016, 2020)
- *Overall premise:* Hollywood is vain, hypocritical and morally compromised, and I'm the only one rude enough to say so to its face. His persona is the outsider who doesn't care.
- **E1. 2020 closer, "no position to lecture."** **[V]** "You're in no position to lecture the public about anything," followed by **[P-mem / widely reported]** "You know nothing about the real world. Most of you spent less time in school than Greta Thunberg. So if you win, come up, accept your little award, thank your agent and your God and f*** off." — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/golden-globes-ricky-gervais-speech-greta-thunberg-jeffrey-epstein/). *Mechanics:* It's presented as practical advice, a mock-helpful rule list. It escalates by insult (no position, then know nothing, then less school than a teenager) and lands on a flip from polite instructions to obscenity. The target is celebrity virtue-signalling. The subject is the acceptance speech.
- **E2. 2020 Apple/ISIS.** **[V]** "Well, you say you're woke but the companies you work for in China — unbelievable. Apple, Amazon, Disney. ### Inferences
- **Recurring bit skeleton (usable template):**
  1. Name a forbidden subject or a rule ("I'm not allowed to joke about X").
  2. Sincere register: compliments, a charitable act, a reading of a sacred text, an "inclusive" qualifier.
  3. Turn by literalism, faux-logic, or a pivot word that swaps the target.
  4. Punch: short, often a single word or a reversal ("Delete." / "the ones with wombs" / "breakfast" / "Why didn't you wish to get better?").
  5. Tags: an act-out of the complainer's voice, a denial tag ("I didn't go 'wake up, baldy'"), a meta tag ("these are all jokes"; "the subject is stereotypes"), and an audience-implication tag ("you laughed at AIDS"; "wouldn't you?").
- **Laugh-line density** comes from tags and act-outs more than from punchlines. In the nut bit the dense laughs come from reading tweets in a voice and the one-word responses.
- **Golden Globes jokes are a separate format:** tight one-liners with real named targets in the room. The persona's freedom ("I don't care anymore") is the premise of the whole set, and the room's discomfort (reaction shots) is a second laugh layer.

## Meta-commentary: deconstructing his own jokes mid-set

### Takeaway
Explaining the joke is a core laugh device for Gervais, not a digression. He steps out of the bit to name its mechanism ("the subject is stereotypes," "this is irony," "these are all jokes"), mock the audience for laughing, or defend himself against imagined critics. The explanation lands in a mock-pompous lecturer voice, and the laugh comes from the pedantry, the implicating of the audience, and the redoubled offence that the "explanation" licenses.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** "The subject of that joke is stereotypes. That's… the target of the joke, just so we're clear." / "And that is why this joke is not transphobic." (Humanity) — [Entertainment Voice](https://entertainmentvoice.com/2018/03/13/nothing-is-sacred-for-ricky-gervais-in-netflix-special-humanity/)
- SuperNature opens with an explanation of irony — [Substack](https://shootingmovies.substack.com/p/ricky-gervais-broke-my-heart); Netflix synopsis covers "the rules of comedy" — [Chortle review page](https://www.chortle.co.uk/review/2022/05/24/50845/ricky_gervais%3A_supernature)

- The audience-implication rebuttal, **[V]** "Yeah, but you laughed about AIDS, the Holocaust…" — [Men's Journal](https://www.mensjournal.com/entertainment/ricky-gervais-opens-up-about-netflix-show-humanity-religion-and-more)

### Inferences
- **Four meta moves to imitate:**
  1. **Pre-framing** (before the joke): "I'm going to say something, and it's ironic." The disclaimer is the joke.
  2. **Post-mortem** (after the laugh): "Now, why did you laugh? The subject was X, the target was Y." Pedantry plays as status comedy.
  3. **Implication** (turning on the room): "You laughed at that, but you're offended by this?"
  4. **Mock-reassurance:** "These are just jokes," said in a way that makes it worse or sets up another dark line.
- The risk, flagged by critics: when meta-defence of past controversy becomes a large share of the set (the 15-minute Jenner section), reviewers read it as grievance rather than craft.

### Gaps
- No source captured a moment where he explains *why* the audience laughed in cognitive terms ("you laughed because of the surprise"). Whether he does this beyond the subject/target lecture is unconfirmed.

## Mechanics summary: escalation, literalism, false logic, sincerity-then-flip, delivery

### Takeaway
His core mechanical toolkit: (1) **literalism**, taking a text, wish or idiom at face value; (2) **faux-logic chains** to a monstrous conclusion; (3) **sincerity-then-flip**, with compliments or charity as setup; (4) **list escalation**; (5) **act-out voices** of complainers; (6) **denial/paralipsis tags**; (7) **meta tags**; (8) delivery: a smug calm, then a giggle that breaks the persona and signals irony.

### Inferences
- **Generator recipe for an original joke in his mode** (inference, not his words):
  1. Pick a high-voltage subject plus a defensible target (a hypocrisy, a stupidity, or your own vanity).
  2. Write a sincere first line in a virtuous register (charity, inclusion, reverence, advice).
  3. Find the **literal reading** or the **logical extreme** of that sincere line.
  4. Compress the punch to the fewest words, ideally a single action or a quoted line ("Delete.").
  5. Add three tags: a voice act-out of the objector, a denial tag that says the worse thing, and a meta tag ("this is irony / the target is X / you laughed at worse").
  6. Keep the persona smug and certain, and let a giggle break it on the darkest tag.
- Worked example (original, inference-based, for illustration only): *Subject:* organ donation. *Target:* my vanity. "I'm on the organ donor register. Obviously. I want to help. I've just put a note on it: 'Only to fans.'" Tag (denial): "I'm not going to make them prove it at the hospital... *Name three episodes of Extras.*" Meta tag: "That's not about sick people. That's about me. Just so we're clear."
- Weak point to avoid: when the declared target is an "ideology" but the act-outs mock a vulnerable group's bodies, critics read the target as the group. The subject/target split only protects a joke whose mechanism points at the stated target.
