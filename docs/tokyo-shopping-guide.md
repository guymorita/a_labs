# Tokyo Shopping Guide — Trendy / Streetwear for a 38yo Guy

**Brief:** Baggy trousers/jeans, cropped boxy tees, chunky knits, cardigans. Trendy but not loud-Gen-Z. Good quality, not crazy expensive. Stuff you can't easily get in the US.

Tokyo's shopping is hyper-neighborhood-specific. Each area has a personality — so the ranking below is a route, not a list. If you only had one day, do **#1 + #2**. If you had three, do the top five.

---

## 1. Shimokitazawa — best all-around starter neighborhood

**Why:** Highest concentration of vintage stores in Tokyo (150+ within walking distance of the station), a young/creative crowd, and prices noticeably lower than Harajuku. This is where current Tokyo 20–30-somethings actually shop. It mixes Americana vintage, '90s/Y2K, and Japanese designer secondhand — exactly the boxy-tee / baggy-pant / cardigan zone.

**Hit these:**
- **Flamingo, New York Joe Exchange, Stick Out, Chicago, Haight & Ashbury** — the big vintage shops. NY Joe and Stick Out are cheap (lots of ¥700–¥2,000 racks). Haight & Ashbury skews curated/designer.
- **2nd STREET Shimokitazawa** — Japan's biggest secondhand chain. Two floors. Mixes Japanese brands you can't get in the US (Beams, Nanamica, Wtaps, Needles) with regular vintage. Expect ¥4,000–¥10,000 for nice pieces.
- **CIRCULABLE SUPPLY Shimokitazawa** — opened April 2025, Baycrew's flagship resale concept. Strong men's vintage Levi's and curated denim.
- **BAZZSTORE** and **KINJI Shimokitazawa** — solid mid-tier vintage chains.

**Pricing:** Vintage tees ¥1,500–¥4,000. Baggy denim ¥4,000–¥9,000. Designer secondhand ¥8,000–¥25,000.
**Vibe:** Walkable, low-key, you can spend a whole afternoon. Good cafes between shops.

---

## 2. Koenji — the deeper cut, fewer tourists, often better prices

**Why:** 200+ vintage stores across two arcades (PAL on the north side, Look on the south). Grittier, older, more "lived-in" than Shimokita. Specialty: Americana, workwear, military surplus, punk/alt — i.e., baggy carpenter pants, chore coats, big knit cardigans, washed boxy tees. If Shimokita is "trendy young Tokyo," Koenji is "stylists and 30-something heads."

**Hit these:**
- **Slat** — strong on US military surplus (cargo pants, M-65 jackets) at fair prices.
- **Whistler** — '40s–'60s American vintage; for the heavyweight knits and chunky cardigan side of the brief.
- **Hayatochiri** — the monster-face storefront; quirkier, colorful pieces.
- **Safari, BIG TIME, Spank!** — local favorites for boxy tees and oversized fits.
- **2nd STREET Koenji** — another big branch, often less picked-over than Shimokita.

**Pricing:** Generally 10–25% cheaper than Shimokitazawa for comparable pieces. Lots of ¥3,000–¥6,000 hits.
**Vibe:** Take the JR Chuo line from Shinjuku, ~7 min. Pair with a cheap izakaya dinner — Koenji's famous for that.

---

## 3. Harajuku / Ura-Hara / Cat Street — current Japanese streetwear brands

**Why:** This is where Japanese streetwear was *invented* (Neighborhood, Undercover, Bape all started here). Skip the Takeshita-dori tourist gauntlet and walk the **back streets (Ura-Harajuku)** and **Cat Street** between Harajuku and Shibuya. That's where the actual brand flagships sit.

**Hit these:**
- **Neighborhood** — moto-biker / Americana / streetwear OG. Heavy flannels, sweats, leather. Quality is excellent; pricing is real (tees ¥8,000–¥15,000, outerwear ¥40,000+).
- **WTAPS, Human Made, Undercover, Visvim, Sacai flagship** — most of these have weak/no US distribution. Going to the actual store gets you Japan-exclusive drops and full size runs.
- **GR8 (in Laforet Harajuku)** — multi-brand select shop carrying Maison MIHARA YASUHIRO, READYMADE, Doublet — Japanese designers that are very hot right now and basically the source for sculpted-baggy silhouettes.
- **Kinji Used Clothing Harajuku** — huge basement floor of vintage, including the weird Japanese school PE uniforms and oversized rugger shirts that go viral on US fashion TikTok.
- **RAGTAG Harajuku** — flagship secondhand designer. 2nd floor is menswear: Bape, Hysteric Glamour, Journal Standard, Comme des Garçons. ¥1,000–¥30,000 range.
- **WEGO Harajuku** — cheap and trend-chasing, but if you want the current Gen Z silhouette (baggy carpenter jean, crop tee, mesh knit) for ¥3,000–¥6,000, this is where Japanese teens actually buy it. Decent quality for the price.

**Pricing:** Wide range. Flagship Japanese streetwear is full price; vintage/select-shop is where the value is.

---

## 4. Shibuya PARCO + MIYASHITA PARK — one-building tour

**Why:** If you only have a few hours, **Shibuya PARCO** is the single highest-density "cool" building in Tokyo. Multiple floors of Japanese streetwear, designer, and Japan-only collabs under one roof. **MIYASHITA PARK** (5 min away) is the slicker, newer sister mall.

**Hit these in PARCO:**
- Comme des Garçons, Sacai, Undercover, Doublet, MIHARA YASUHIRO, Needles, South2 West8 — many have store-exclusive pieces here.
- **The Conveni / Fragment-adjacent pop-ups** — rotating drops; fun even if you don't buy.
- **2G** (curated streetwear/designer mix on the 2nd floor).

**Hit these in MIYASHITA PARK:**
- **KITH Tokyo** — yes you can get Kith in NY, but the Tokyo store carries Japan-only collabs.
- **New Era flagship** — Japan-exclusive caps.
- **Coach, Prada Sport, Louis Vuitton Men's** — if you want to see the luxury-streetwear crossover Tokyo does best.

**Pricing:** Full retail. This is "see what's current and what fits," then go hunt the same brand secondhand elsewhere if you want to save.

---

## 5. Daikanyama + Nakameguro — the grown-up version of all the above

**Why:** This is where Tokyo's 30–40-somethings with taste shop. Tree-lined, calm, no crowds. Selection skews "elevated baggy" — perfect for your age bracket. You won't look like you're cosplaying a teenager.

**Hit these:**
- **Okura (Daikanyama)** — Blue Blue Japan flagship. Indigo-dyed knits, sashiko jackets, heavyweight tees. Made-in-Japan, ¥10,000–¥40,000 range. Genuinely hard to get in the US.
- **BEAMS Daikanyama / Beauty & Youth UA** — see #6.
- **Coverchord (Nakameguro)** — riverside multi-brand for men, women, home. Very current Japanese designers.
- **Forget Me Nots (Daikanyama)** — Courrèges, Marine Serre, Ganni — Euro brands curated through a Tokyo lens.
- **2nd STREET Nakameguro** — bigger, more designer-heavy than the Shimokita branch.

**Pricing:** Mid-to-upper. ¥10,000–¥50,000 sweet spot. Worth it for pieces you'll wear for years.

---

## 6. Select shops you can hit anywhere (the BEAMS / UA universe)

These are Japan's "select shop" big three — multi-brand stores that mix their own house label with the best Japanese + international brands. They are *the* one-stop solution if you want quality baggy Japanese-cut basics without dedicating a day to vintage hunting.

- **BEAMS Men Shibuya** — flagship men's store. Carries Cav Empt (C.E.), Needles, South2 West8, Engineered Garments, plus the **BEAMS T** in-house graphic tee line (this is the boxy-tee jackpot; ¥4,000–¥7,000 per tee, lots of artist collabs).
- **United Arrows Harajuku flagship** — slightly more grown-up tailored-streetwear.
- **BEAUTY & YOUTH UNITED ARROWS (Harajuku, Shinjuku, Shibuya)** — younger/edgier UA line, very on-trend baggy silhouettes.
- **H BEAUTY & YOUTH (Minami-Aoyama)** — the high-end concept store. Worth it just to see the merchandising.
- **nanamica Tokyo (Daikanyama-ish)** — Japan's better-than-Patagonia outdoor-meets-streetwear brand. Almost no US presence.

**Pricing:** House-brand items ¥6,000–¥20,000 are the value zone. Imported designer pieces are full retail.

---

## 7. Secondhand designer chains (drop in opportunistically)

These have branches all over Tokyo — if you're walking past one, pop in. 10 minutes can turn up a ¥6,000 Comme des Garçons cardigan.

- **2nd STREET** — biggest chain, 70+ Tokyo locations. Categorized into Apparel Reuse / Used Select / Brand Specialty. Best general-purpose stop.
- **RAGTAG** — Harajuku and Shibuya branches. Curated secondhand designer: Bape, Hysteric Glamour, Comme des Garçons, Margiela.
- **Kindal** — has a men-only branch right at Shibuya crossing. Strong on Margiela, Comme des Garçons, Louis Vuitton, plus rare sneakers.

**Pricing:** ¥1,000 surprises up to ¥30,000 for archival pieces.

## Brands worth hunting specifically (limited/no US distribution)

If you see these, look hard — they're the "you can't get this back home" payoff:

- **Needles** (Nepenthes) — track pants and butterfly-embroidered everything. Cult in the US, much cheaper and fuller selection in Tokyo.
- **Cav Empt / C.E.** — Sk8thing's brand. Boxy graphic tees, washed knits.
- **Human Made** — Nigo's post-Bape label.
- **WTAPS** — military-influenced cuts; the baggy cargo source.
- **Visvim** — pricey but archetypal Japanese craft-streetwear.
- **YOKE** — newer, quiet-luxury Japanese; soft tailoring with relaxed silhouettes.
- **SS Stein** — minimal avant-garde; oversized/relaxed.
- **Doublet, MIHARA YASUHIRO, sacai, Undercover, Comme des Garçons Homme Plus** — the designer end of the same vibe.
- **Maison MIHARA YASUHIRO sneakers** — the "melted Converse" look that's everywhere right now.
- **HARE** — Japanese mass-market with proper baggy/wide silhouettes, very affordable (¥4,000–¥12,000). Tons of branches.
- **GU** — Uniqlo's cheaper, trendier sibling. Worth a 30-min stop. The current baggy chinos / cropped tees are ¥2,000–¥4,000 and surprisingly fine.

## Practical notes

- **Tax-free shopping:** Bring your passport. Spend ¥5,000+ at most fashion retailers and you get ~10% off at checkout. (Rules tightened in 2026 — confirm at the register, but the threshold and refund concept are the same.)
- **Sizing:** Japanese L ≈ US M, sometimes US S. Try things on. The baggy/oversized cuts you want are *designed* baggy, so they're often roomy at true-to-size.
- **Cash vs. card:** Most shops take card now, but smaller vintage spots may still be cash-preferred.
- **Timing:** Most shops open at 11:00 or 12:00, not 10:00. Don't show up early. Many smaller vintage shops close one weekday — check before traveling across town.
- **Order to do them in if you have 3 days:**
  - Day 1: Shimokitazawa (afternoon → dinner)
  - Day 2: Harajuku/Cat Street → walk to Shibuya PARCO → MIYASHITA PARK
  - Day 3: Koenji (morning, fewer crowds) → Daikanyama/Nakameguro (afternoon)
