# Tokyo Shopping Guide — Trendy / Streetwear for a 38yo Guy

**Brief:** Baggy trousers/jeans, cropped boxy tees, chunky knits, cardigans. Trendy but not loud-Gen-Z. Good quality, not crazy expensive. Stuff you can't easily get in the US.

**You're staying in:** Shinjuku — perfectly central. Every neighborhood below is 5–20 min by train.

Tokyo's shopping is hyper-neighborhood-specific. Each area has a personality — so the ranking below is a route, not a list. If you only had one day, do **#1 + #2**. If you had three, do the top five.

> Map links are Google Maps search URLs — they'll open in the app on your phone and drop the pin on the right place.

## 1. Shimokitazawa — best all-around starter neighborhood

**Where:** Shimokitazawa Station (下北沢).
**From Shinjuku:** Odakyu line, ~7 min (¥170). Direct.
**Area map:** [Shimokitazawa on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Shimokitazawa+Tokyo)

**Why:** Highest concentration of vintage stores in Tokyo (150+ within walking distance of the station), a young/creative crowd, and prices noticeably lower than Harajuku. This is where current Tokyo 20–30-somethings actually shop. It mixes Americana vintage, '90s/Y2K, and Japanese designer secondhand — exactly the boxy-tee / baggy-pant / cardigan zone.

**Hit these:**
- **Flamingo Shimokitazawa** — big vintage, Americana/Y2K. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Flamingo+Shimokitazawa)
- **New York Joe Exchange** — cheap vintage, ¥700+ racks. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=New+York+Joe+Exchange+Shimokitazawa)
- **Stick Out** — flat-rate (~¥700) vintage. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Stick+Out+Shimokitazawa)
- **Chicago Shimokitazawa** — huge, all-purpose vintage. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Chicago+Shimokitazawa)
- **Haight & Ashbury** — curated designer vintage (CdG, Margiela). [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Haight+and+Ashbury+Shimokitazawa)
- **2nd STREET Shimokitazawa** — Japan's biggest secondhand chain. Mixes Japanese brands you can't get in the US (Beams, Nanamica, Wtaps, Needles) with regular vintage. ¥4,000–¥10,000 for nice pieces. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=2nd+STREET+Shimokitazawa)
- **CIRCULABLE SUPPLY Shimokitazawa** — opened April 2025, Baycrew's resale concept. Strong men's vintage Levi's. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=CIRCULABLE+SUPPLY+Shimokitazawa)
- **BAZZSTORE Shimokitazawa** — solid mid-tier vintage. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=BAZZSTORE+Shimokitazawa)
- **KINJI Shimokitazawa** — Y2K, branded vintage. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=KINJI+Shimokitazawa)

**Pricing:** Vintage tees ¥1,500–¥4,000. Baggy denim ¥4,000–¥9,000. Designer secondhand ¥8,000–¥25,000.
**Vibe:** Walkable, low-key, can spend a whole afternoon. Good cafes between shops.

## 2. Koenji — the deeper cut, fewer tourists, often better prices

**Where:** Kōenji Station (高円寺).
**From Shinjuku:** JR Chuo line (orange, rapid) or Sobu line (yellow, local), ~5–7 min (¥160).
**Area map:** [Koenji on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Koenji+Tokyo)

**Why:** 200+ vintage stores across two arcades — **PAL Shopping Street** (north exit) and **Look Shopping Street** (south exit). Grittier, older, more "lived-in" than Shimokita. Specialty: Americana, workwear, military surplus, punk/alt — i.e., baggy carpenter pants, chore coats, big knit cardigans, washed boxy tees. If Shimokita is "trendy young Tokyo," Koenji is "stylists and 30-something heads."

**Hit these:**
- **PAL Shopping Street (arcade)** — north side. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Koenji+PAL+Shopping+Street)
- **Look Shopping Street (arcade)** — south side. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Koenji+Look+Shopping+Street)
- **Slat Koenji** — US military surplus, cargo pants, M-65 jackets. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Slat+Koenji)
- **Whistler Koenji** — '40s–'60s American vintage; heavyweight knits, chunky cardigans. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Whistler+Koenji+vintage)
- **Hayatochiri Koenji** — quirky, colorful, the monster-face storefront. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Hayatochiri+Koenji)
- **Safari Koenji** — local fave for oversized fits. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Safari+Koenji+vintage)
- **BIG TIME Koenji** — boxy tees, Y2K. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=BIG+TIME+Koenji)
- **Spank! Koenji** — colorful Y2K / cute-grunge. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Spank+Koenji)
- **2nd STREET Koenji** — another big branch, often less picked-over than Shimokita. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=2nd+STREET+Koenji)

**Pricing:** Generally 10–25% cheaper than Shimokitazawa for comparable pieces. Lots of ¥3,000–¥6,000 hits.
**Vibe:** Pair with a cheap izakaya dinner — Koenji's famous for that.

## 3. Harajuku / Ura-Hara / Cat Street — current Japanese streetwear brands

**Where:** Harajuku Station (原宿) or Meiji-jingumae Station (明治神宮前).
**From Shinjuku:** JR Yamanote line, ~5 min (¥160) to Harajuku. Or Fukutoshin line ~3 min to Meiji-jingumae.
**Area maps:** [Ura-Harajuku](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ura+Harajuku) · [Cat Street](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Cat+Street+Harajuku)

**Why:** This is where Japanese streetwear was *invented* (Neighborhood, Undercover, Bape all started here). Skip the Takeshita-dori tourist gauntlet and walk the **back streets (Ura-Harajuku)** and **Cat Street** between Harajuku and Shibuya. That's where the actual brand flagships sit.

**Hit these:**
- **Neighborhood Harajuku** — moto-biker / Americana / streetwear OG. Tees ¥8,000–¥15,000. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Neighborhood+Harajuku+store)
- **WTAPS GIP-Tokyo** — military-influenced cuts; baggy cargo source. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=WTAPS+GIP+Tokyo)
- **Human Made 1928 Shibuya/Harajuku** — Nigo's post-Bape label. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Human+Made+1928+Shibuya)
- **Undercover Aoyama** — Jun Takahashi flagship, walking distance. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Undercover+Aoyama)
- **Visvim F.I.L. Tokyo** — Daikanyama-side, but same trip. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Visvim+FIL+Tokyo)
- **sacai flagship Aoyama** — [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=sacai+Aoyama+flagship)
- **GR8 (inside Laforet Harajuku)** — multi-brand select shop carrying MIHARA YASUHIRO, READYMADE, Doublet. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Laforet+Harajuku)
- **Kinji Used Clothing Harajuku** — huge basement vintage floor; weird Japanese PE uniforms, oversized rugger shirts. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kinji+Harajuku)
- **RAGTAG Harajuku** — secondhand designer flagship. 2nd floor menswear: Bape, Hysteric Glamour, CdG. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=RAGTAG+Harajuku)
- **WEGO Harajuku** — cheap, current Gen Z silhouettes for ¥3,000–¥6,000. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=WEGO+Harajuku)

**Pricing:** Wide range. Flagship Japanese streetwear is full price; vintage/select-shop is where the value is.

## 4. Shibuya PARCO + MIYASHITA PARK — one-building tour

**Where:** Shibuya Station (渋谷). Both are a 5–8 min walk from the station, and 5 min apart from each other.
**From Shinjuku:** JR Yamanote line, ~7 min (¥160). Or Fukutoshin line, ~7 min.
**Maps:** [Shibuya PARCO](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Shibuya+PARCO) · [MIYASHITA PARK](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=MIYASHITA+PARK+Shibuya)

**Why:** If you only have a few hours, **Shibuya PARCO** is the single highest-density "cool" building in Tokyo. Multiple floors of Japanese streetwear, designer, and Japan-only collabs under one roof. **MIYASHITA PARK** is the slicker, newer sister mall.

**Hit these in PARCO:**
- Comme des Garçons, sacai, Undercover, Doublet, MIHARA YASUHIRO, Needles, South2 West8 — many have store-exclusive pieces.
- **The Conveni / Fragment-adjacent pop-ups** — rotating drops.
- **2G** (curated streetwear/designer mix on the 2nd floor).

**Hit these in MIYASHITA PARK:**
- **KITH Tokyo** — Japan-only collabs even though Kith exists in NY. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=KITH+Tokyo+Miyashita+Park)
- **New Era flagship** — Japan-exclusive caps. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=New+Era+Miyashita+Park)
- **Coach, Prada Sport, Louis Vuitton Men's** — Tokyo-style luxury-streetwear crossover.

**Pricing:** Full retail. This is "see what's current and what fits," then go hunt the same brand secondhand elsewhere if you want to save.

## 5. Daikanyama + Nakameguro — the grown-up version of all the above

**Where:** Daikanyama Station (代官山) and Nakameguro Station (中目黒). 10 min walk apart.
**From Shinjuku:** JR Yamanote to Shibuya (~7 min), then Tokyu Toyoko line one stop to Daikanyama (~2 min). Total ~15 min, ¥320.
**Maps:** [Daikanyama](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Daikanyama+Tokyo) · [Nakameguro](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Nakameguro+Tokyo)

**Why:** This is where Tokyo's 30–40-somethings with taste shop. Tree-lined, calm, no crowds. Selection skews "elevated baggy" — perfect for your age bracket. You won't look like you're cosplaying a teenager.

**Hit these:**
- **Okura (Daikanyama)** — Blue Blue Japan flagship. Indigo-dyed knits, sashiko jackets, heavyweight tees. ¥10,000–¥40,000. Genuinely hard to get in the US. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Okura+Daikanyama+Blue+Blue+Japan)
- **BEAMS Daikanyama** — see #6. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=BEAMS+Daikanyama)
- **Coverchord Nakameguro** — riverside multi-brand for men, women, home. Current Japanese designers. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Coverchord+Nakameguro)
- **Forget Me Nots Daikanyama** — Courrèges, Marine Serre, Ganni — Euro brands through a Tokyo lens. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Forget+Me+Nots+Daikanyama)
- **2nd STREET Nakameguro** — bigger, more designer-heavy than the Shimokita branch. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=2nd+STREET+Nakameguro)

**Pricing:** Mid-to-upper. ¥10,000–¥50,000 sweet spot. Worth it for pieces you'll wear for years.

## 6. Select shops you can hit anywhere (the BEAMS / UA universe)

Japan's "select shop" big three mix their own house label with the best Japanese + international brands. The one-stop solution if you want quality baggy Japanese-cut basics without dedicating a day to vintage hunting.

- **BEAMS Men Shibuya** — flagship men's store. Cav Empt (C.E.), Needles, South2 West8, Engineered Garments, plus **BEAMS T** (the boxy-tee jackpot; ¥4,000–¥7,000, lots of artist collabs). [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=BEAMS+Men+Shibuya)
- **BEAMS Japan Shinjuku** — 5 min walk from your hotel. Multi-floor; ground floor has the latest seasonal stuff. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=BEAMS+JAPAN+Shinjuku)
- **United Arrows Harajuku flagship** — slightly more grown-up tailored-streetwear. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=United+Arrows+Harajuku+flagship)
- **BEAUTY & YOUTH UNITED ARROWS Shinjuku** — younger/edgier UA line, on-trend baggy silhouettes. Walking distance from your hotel. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=BEAUTY+AND+YOUTH+UNITED+ARROWS+Shinjuku)
- **H BEAUTY & YOUTH Minami-Aoyama** — the high-end concept store. Worth it just to see the merchandising. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=H+BEAUTY+AND+YOUTH+Minami+Aoyama)
- **nanamica Tokyo (Daikanyama)** — Japan's better-than-Patagonia outdoor-meets-streetwear. Almost no US presence. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=nanamica+Tokyo+Daikanyama)

**Pricing:** House-brand items ¥6,000–¥20,000 are the value zone.

## 7. Secondhand designer chains (drop in opportunistically)

These have branches all over Tokyo. 10 minutes can turn up a ¥6,000 Comme des Garçons cardigan.

- **2nd STREET** — biggest chain, 70+ Tokyo locations. Best general-purpose stop. [Find nearest](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=2nd+STREET+Tokyo)
- **RAGTAG** — Harajuku, Shibuya, Shinjuku branches. Curated secondhand designer: Bape, Hysteric Glamour, CdG, Margiela. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=RAGTAG+Tokyo)
- **Kindal** — men-only branch right at Shibuya crossing. Strong on Margiela, CdG, LV, rare sneakers. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kindal+Shibuya+men)

**Pricing:** ¥1,000 surprises up to ¥30,000 for archival pieces.

## Practical notes

- **Tax-free:** Bring your passport. Spend ¥5,000+ at most fashion retailers for ~10% off at checkout. Rules tightened in 2026 — confirm at the register.
- **Sizing:** Japanese L ≈ US M, sometimes US S. Try things on. Baggy/oversized cuts are *designed* baggy, so they're often roomy at true-to-size.
- **Cash vs. card:** Most shops take card now, but smaller vintage spots may still be cash-preferred.
- **Timing:** Most shops open at 11:00 or 12:00, not 10:00. Don't show up early. Many smaller vintage shops close one weekday — check before traveling.
- **Suggested 3-day route from Shinjuku:**
  - **Day 1:** Shimokitazawa (afternoon → dinner). Odakyu line, 7 min.
  - **Day 2:** Harajuku/Cat Street → walk to Shibuya PARCO → MIYASHITA PARK. Yamanote line, 5 min to start.
  - **Day 3:** Koenji (morning, fewer crowds; Chuo line, 7 min) → Daikanyama/Nakameguro (afternoon).
  - **Any spare hour:** Isetan Men's + BEAMS Japan, walking distance from your hotel.
